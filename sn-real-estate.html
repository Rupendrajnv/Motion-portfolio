01 · The businessWho they are

Context

A real estate company
that runs on trust.

S&N buys run-down homes, fixes them up and puts them back on the market. The money for it comes from everyday people who lend the company their savings.

Those people are teachers, doctors and middle-class families, not Wall Street. Nobody hands their savings to a company they don't trust. So every piece of content had one job: earn trust before asking for anything.

02 · The first decisionAudience

The decision

Stop talking to everyone.
Talk to the saver.

Before

Content for anyone interested in real estate.

Broad tips that could be for anybody, so they spoke to nobody in particular.

After

Content for one person: a saver with money sitting in the bank.

Teachers, doctors, middle-class households. Plain words, no finance jargon, trust before the ask.

The hook this led to

“Is your money sleeping in the bank?”

It talks to the saver directly, names their problem in their own words, and doesn't try to sell anything in the first line.

06 · A problem I causedApprovals

What went wrong

My approval system
slowed everyone down.

What I built

A Google Sheet and a Google Doc.

The sheet tracked every piece. The doc held scripts and feedback. Neat, on paper.

Why it failed

He reviewed on his phone.

The founder checked work on his phone and tablet. To approve anything he had to open a laptop, and juggling two files was confusing.

The fix

Move it to Adobe Express.

The company already had Adobe Express through Creative Cloud. I taught myself how to use it, then moved approvals and content scheduling into it.

The lesson

Build the workflow around how the reviewer actually works, not how I like to work.
